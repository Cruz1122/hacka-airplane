# Evidence notes — health_safety

Consultation date for all pages: 2026-11-21. These are concise notes from pages actually opened and read, not copied page archives.

- Parques Nacionales, PNN Los Nevados, https://www.parquesnacionales.gov.co/nuestros-parques/parque-nacional-natural-los-nevados/ — lines 108–115: asks visitors to ensure suitable health for high mountain, bring basic equipment, enter with a registered operator/guide and accident insurance. Publication date not displayed in the opened page. Supports `high_altitude_health_risk`, `pretrip_medical_advice`, `tell_guide_about_health_change`; source evidence supports general context only, not clinical diagnosis.
- CDC Yellow Book, “High-Altitude Travel and Altitude Illness,” https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/environmental-hazards-risks/high-altitude-travel-and-altitude-illness.html — dated 2025-04-23, 2026 edition. Lines 98–108: acute mountain sickness includes headache with nausea, dizziness, fatigue or appetite loss; lines 204–213: do not ascend with symptoms, descend if worsening despite rest, pre-existing conditions warrant clinician input. Supports altitude risk, symptoms, warning signs, no-ascent, descent and pretravel advice.
- CDC Travelers’ Health, “Travel to High Altitudes,” https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/page/travel-to-high-altitudes — last reviewed 2022-08-16. Lines 30–65: symptoms, severe cerebral/pulmonary warning signs and medical consultation for existing conditions. Supports `altitude_sickness_symptoms`, `severe_altitude_warning_signs`, `no_ascend_while_symptomatic`, `descent_for_worsening_altitude`, `pretrip_medical_advice`.
- CDC Yellow Book, “Traveling Safely with Infants and Children,” https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/family-travel/traveling-safely-with-infants-and-children.html — opened at lines 179–181; children are susceptible like adults; younger children can show fussiness, appetite loss or sleep/activity changes, and descent may be necessary for unexplained post-ascent symptoms. The page’s publication date was not visible in the returned excerpt. Supports `children_altitude_signs`.
- CDC Travelers’ Health, “Cold Weather and Travel,” https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/page/travel-to-cold-climates/ — last reviewed 2022-12-12. Lines 41–63: hypothermia signs include shivering, tiredness, clumsiness and confusion; urgent medical attention; shelter, remove wet clothing and warm gradually; do not give drinks to unconscious person. Lines 65–73: frostbite can cause numbness, tingling or pain; seek care and move to shelter. Supports hypothermia and frostbite entries.
- CDC NIOSH, “Cold-related Illnesses in Workers,” https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/cold-stress/about/related-illness.html — page dated 2026-03-03. Lines 42–65 and 88–107 detail hypothermia/frostbite signs and first aid; supports `hypothermia_warning_signs`, `hypothermia_first_response`, `frostbite_warning_signs`.
- CDC Travelers’ Health, “Sun Exposure,” https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/page/sun-exposure — opened on 2026-11-21, no page review date in returned content. Lines 19–45: high elevation, equatorial location, clouds/cold and reflective snow increase UV concern; broad-spectrum sunscreen, covering clothing and UV-blocking sunglasses are protective. Supports `sun_uv_protection`.
- MedlinePlus, “Dehydration,” https://medlineplus.gov/dehydration.html — opened on 2026-11-21. Lines 96–124 give adult/child symptoms and severe red flags; lines 137–148 discuss fluid replacement and seeking care. Supports `dehydration_warning_signs`.
- Policía Nacional de Colombia, “La Policía Nacional sensibiliza a la comunidad sobre el uso de las líneas de emergencia 123 y 155,” https://www.policia.gov.co/noticia/policia-nacional-sensibiliza-comunidad-sobre-uso-las-lineas-emergencia-123-155 — dated 2023-03-12. Lines 19–26 identify 123 as the national emergency line and state it is free and available all hours. Supports `national_emergency_number_123`. Coverage and dispatch response inside remote park terrain are not guaranteed by this source.
- Parques Nacionales, “Decálogo de recomendaciones para el uso de áreas protegidas,” https://old.parquesnacionales.gov.co/portal/es/decalogo-de-recomendaciones-para-el-uso-de-areas-protegidas-en-parques-nacionales-naturales-de-colombia/ — page reports update 2026-06-19, legacy page; lines 45–54 recommend suitable preparation, carrying prescribed medication/medical items, attending induction and following safety recommendations.
